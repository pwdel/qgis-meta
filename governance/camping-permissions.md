# Camping permission and permit zones

Keep `winter_policy` and `nps_permit` (or the applicable manager's permit field) independent. `required` describes a condition of allowed camping, not a prohibited status. Never assign the red prohibited X solely because a permit, fee or reservation is required. Preserve explicit day-use prohibitions and seasonal closures.

Each project must retain the managing authority, geographic endpoints, eligible site type, seasonal permission, permit requirement, stay limits, official source URLs and review date in its research rules. Prefer the specific permit scope over overview shorthand. For St. Croix, “below Highway 8” must not silently extend past Soo Line High Bridge. State park permits are separate.

A published atlas must contain the applicable rule summaries and readable source links, with a pointer from the map key. Give rule zones distinct colors and textual labels. Distinguish an illustrative corridor from surveyed legal boundaries and actual campsite eligibility. Keep ownership/easement data separate from camping permission.

Project acceptance tests must exercise both allowed-with-permit and allowed-without-permit cases; preserve prohibited locations; confirm all published zones reference official rules; and verify the rules page and map legend survive project/template reopening. The generic validator checks rendering and dependencies, not the legal interpretation of a source.

## Exceptions and planning exclusions

Use a circled red X (`assets/symbols/restrictions/special-authorization.svg`) for a general prohibition with a discretionary special-authorization pathway. Retain the default prohibition, the exact authority and whether any authorization is actually held. This is distinct from an ordinary recreational camping permit. Do not label an activity “scientific camping allowed” solely because an application pathway exists.

Use the boxed red X (`assets/symbols/restrictions/user-excluded.svg`) for a user's planning exclusion. Its legend must identify it as a user choice. Preserve the underlying legal status; “too urban” does not establish a statutory camping prohibition.

A designated parking area, including permission to leave a vehicle overnight while camping elsewhere, does not establish that the parking lot or adjacent trail is a designated campsite. Apply street ordinances only to their stated geographic and temporal scope, retaining posted-sign requirements and police-approval exceptions. Representative municipal annotations must say they are not surveyed parking facilities.
